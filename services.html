Services

We match the method to the question, the soil and the ground cover, and often combine two or more on one project. Every report says in plain language what was found, how confident we are, and what the data cannot tell you.

Magnetometry

  • Equipment: multi-sensor ground carts (Sensys and Bartington), a MagDrone flown by drone, and drone magnetometry over water for maritime projects. Every reading has a precise GPS position.
  • Finds: burned features (hearths, kilns, burned houses), pits, ditches, wall trenches, fired clay and iron.
  • Limits: features with no magnetic contrast, or deeper than about a metre or two. Fences, power lines and modern iron hide what is near them.
  • Best for: the first look at large open areas; the fastest way to map a whole site or landscape.

Ground-penetrating radar (GPR)

  • Equipment: GSSI radar systems. Results are map views at a series of depths.
  • Finds: walls, floors, foundations, voids, buried surfaces, pits and grave shafts, with estimated depth.
  • Limits: wet clay and salty soil absorb the signal. GPR shows that something is different below ground, not what it is.
  • Best for: detailed work on smaller areas, under pavement and in towns, in cemeteries, and wherever depth matters.

Drone lidar

  • Equipment: drone laser scanning, filtered to a bare-earth model, even under many kinds of tree cover.
  • Finds: mounds, earthworks, ditches, old roads, house depressions and field boundaries.
  • Limits: only the shape of the ground, nothing below it. Very dense brush can leave gaps.
  • Best for: mounds and earthworks, wooded sites, whole landscapes, and a base map for the other methods.

EM and earth resistance

  • Finds: stone walls and foundations, ditches, and changes in soil and moisture.
  • Limits: small objects. Earth resistance is slow and needs good ground contact, so very dry or hard ground is difficult.
  • Best for: alongside magnetometry, where features are stone or magnetic readings are too noisy.

Cemetery investigations

Unmarked-grave searches for cemetery boards, churches, families, agencies and developers: checking that a planned construction area is clear of graves, finding a cemetery's edge, or locating graves whose markers are gone.

  • Shows (GPR and magnetometry): grave-shaped areas of disturbed soil, some coffins and burial vaults, and metal coffin hardware.
  • Limits: not bones. Results are possible graves; only excavation can confirm a burial. Clay soils, tree roots and earlier disturbance can hide graves or look like them.
  • Best for: before construction near a known or suspected cemetery, and old cemeteries with incomplete records.

Military recovery (MIA) projects

AGA provides support for military recovery (MIA) projects, using the same methods to narrow down where excavation teams should look.

Data processing and GIS

AGA processes its own data with tested, documented tools, and delivers results as maps, GIS layers and plain-language reports. We also process data collected by others.
